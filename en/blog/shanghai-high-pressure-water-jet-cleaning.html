1. Shanghai's Industrial Landscape and Jetting Demand

As China's largest economic center, Shanghai hosts dense Grade-A office towers and commercial complexes whose HVAC chilled-water and cooling-water lines scale and foul over time, reducing cooling capacity and raising energy costs. Lingang, Jiading and Jinqiao concentrate advanced manufacturing where process cooling lines and heat exchangers demand high cleanliness. The shipyards of Changxing Island build new vessels whose lines must be cleared of weld slag, rust and oil before delivery. The Shanghai Chemical Industry Park in Jinshan and Caojing sees reactors, heat exchangers and process pipelines prone to coking, scaling and blockage. These are exactly the scenarios where high-pressure water jetting excels.

High-pressure water jetting pressurizes water to hundreds or even thousands of bar and ejects it through specialized nozzles into high-energy jets. Impact, wedging and shearing strip hard scale, rust and blockages from pipe and vessel walls. Unlike chemical cleaning, it introduces no agents, produces no acid or alkali effluent, and barely damages the substrate, making it ideal for stainless steel, titanium and enamel surfaces, as well as silicate, cement and polymer deposits that chemicals cannot dissolve.

2. Technical Approach

2.1 Pressure Grading and Nozzle Selection

Pressure is chosen by scale hardness and material: low (200-400 bar) for soft scale and biofilm in heat-exchanger tubes, condenser copper tubes and HVAC lines; medium (400-700 bar) for hard scale, rust and paint in industrial pipes; high (700-1000 bar and above) for blocked heat-exchanger tubes, reactor polymer coke and marine-line weld slag. Forward, rear, rotary and fan nozzles, combined with self-advancing hoses, deliver full 360° coverage even in long, elbow-heavy pipelines.

2.2 Industrial Pipes and Heat Exchangers

For scaled or blocked shell-and-tube heat-exchanger bundles, rotary high-pressure jets clear each tube to restore flow and temperature difference. Process pipelines are cleaned with dedicated pipeline nozzles on flexible hoses to remove internal hard scale, rust and coke, restoring flow and pressure drop.

2.3 Marine Lines and HVAC Lines

New-ship line systems are cleared of weld slag, rust and oil before passivation and anti-rust treatment. HVAC chilled-water and cooling-water lines are flushed with high-pressure jets plus flexible-shaft cleaning to remove scale, algae and sludge, restoring cooling delivery and lowering chiller energy use.

2.4 Municipal and Drainage

Stormwater pipes, sewage pipes, settling tanks and cooling-tower fill are unblocked by high-pressure jetting to restore drainage and reduce downtime and overflow risk.

5. FAQ

Q1: Will high-pressure water jetting damage the equipment?

No. Pressure and nozzle are matched precisely to the material and scale hardness, concentrating energy on the scale layer with little effect on the base metal. For stainless steel, titanium and copper, jetting is safer than chemical cleaning.

Q2: What equipment suits high-pressure water jetting?

A wide range: heat-exchanger and condenser bundles, process pipelines, HVAC chilled/cooling-water lines, new-ship lines, reactor and tank interiors, municipal drains and cooling-tower fill. Any scaled, blocked, coked or rusted surface that should avoid chemicals suits jetting.

Q3: How to choose between jetting and chemical cleaning?

Acid-soluble calcium carbonate scale is efficiently handled by chemical cleaning over large areas; silicate, cement, polymer coke and fully blocked tubes need jetting. Sensitive materials like stainless steel and titanium favor jetting. Most projects combine both for best results.

Q4: How is cleaning pressure determined?

Engineers determine it on site from scale hardness, material, pipe diameter and deposit thickness, choosing 200-1000 bar with matching nozzles. The principle is to strip the scale without harming the substrate — higher is not always better.
