1. Drivers of Green Cleaning

Industrial equipment chemical cleaning has long relied on strong acids and alkalis such as HCl, HNO₃, and NaOH. While highly effective at scale removal, they carry high wastewater treatment costs, significant corrosion risk, and serious operator safety concerns. Three developments are accelerating industry transformation:

First, tightening environmental regulation. Discharge standards for heavy-metal-containing and strong-acid wastewater continue to rise, causing corporate wastewater treatment costs to soar — some chemical parks have already restricted traditional acid cleaning as a controlled process. Second, ESG disclosure pressure. Listed companies and foreign-invested supply chains are setting quantified green production targets, with equipment cleaning as a high-frequency maintenance item now a key audit focus. Third, technology maturity. Biodegradable organic acids represented by Citric Acid, after 25 years of engineering validation, can now equivalently replace inorganic acids in most operating conditions.

2. Major Types of Biodegradable Cleaning Agents

2.1 Organic Acids

Citric Acid is the most widely used biodegradable acid cleaning agent today. Its descaling mechanism involves reacting with CaCO₃ and MgCO₃ to form soluble citrates, while chelating iron ions to prevent secondary precipitation. Biodegradation rate exceeds 90% (OECD 301B), far surpassing inorganic acids. It is suitable for stainless steel, copper alloys, aluminum, and other metals sensitive to Cl⁻. Typical use concentration: 3%-8%, temperature: 60-80°C.

Other commonly used organic acids include Glycolic Acid, Lactic Acid, and Gluconic Acid, each with specific applicable scenarios. Glycolic Acid, with its small molecular size and strong penetration, is effective on dense scale layers; Gluconic Acid has outstanding iron-ion chelation capacity and is often used in post-alkaline rinse stages.

2.2 Enzyme Cleaners

Enzyme cleaners leverage the specific catalytic properties of biological enzymes to degrade targeted organic substances. The main types include:

Enzyme Type Target Suitable Equipment Optimal Temp / pH
ProteaseProtein depositsDairy plate heat exchangers, pharmaceutical reactors40-55°C / pH 7-9
LipaseOils, fatty acidsFood frying equipment, grease traps35-50°C / pH 6-8
AmylaseStarch depositsStarch processing lines, fermentation tanks50-65°C / pH 5.5-7
CellulaseBiofilms, celluloseCooling tower fill, membrane system pretreatment40-55°C / pH 4.5-6

The core advantages of enzyme cleaning are room-temperature operation, zero metal corrosion, and non-toxic wastewater. Limitations include slow reaction speed (2-6 hours soaking required), ineffectiveness against inorganic scale, and temperature sensitivity (enzymes denature above 70°C). In engineering practice, enzyme cleaning is often sequenced with chemical cleaning: enzymes first degrade organic deposits, followed by organic acids to dissolve inorganic scale.

2.3 Plant-Based Surfactants

Traditional surfactants like NP-10 (nonylphenol ethoxylate), which degrade slowly and have endocrine-disrupting effects, are being replaced by plant-based alternatives such as Alkyl Polyglucoside (APG) and Cocamidopropyl Betaine. APG, synthesized from glucose and fatty alcohols, has a biodegradation rate exceeding 98% and is used in industrial cleaning as a penetrant and emulsifier — particularly effective for pretreatment of oil and mixed-fouling deposits.

2.4 Biodegradable Chelating Aids

Sodium Gluconate and Sodium Citrate are commonly used biodegradable chelating dispersants. They replace STPP (sodium tripolyphosphate) in alkaline cleaning formulations, avoiding phosphorus discharge that causes water eutrophication. They are particularly suitable for online cleaning and pre-filming treatment of circulating cooling water systems.

4. Typical Application Scenarios

4.1 Food & Beverage CIP System

A beverage manufacturer's stainless steel fermentation and storage tank CIP system originally used alternating 2% NaOH + 1% HNO₃ cleaning, with wastewater requiring off-site disposal. Switching to 2% Citric Acid + 0.5% APG maintained equivalent cleaning results while meeting discharge standards — annual wastewater disposal costs dropped by approximately 80,000 CNY. The HNO₃-related NOx exhaust issue was also eliminated, significantly improving workshop air quality.

4.2 Stainless Steel Heat Exchanger Online Cleaning

A chemical plant's stainless steel plate heat exchanger experienced a 40% drop in heat transfer efficiency due to CaCO₃ scaling. Using 8% Citric Acid + 0.3% BTA inhibitor with 8-hour circulation achieved 96% descaling at 0.8 g/(m²·h) corrosion rate — far better than the 2.5 g/(m²·h) of the HCl alternative. Post-cleaning, the temperature differential recovered to 95% of design, with no Cl⁻ residual stress corrosion cracking concern.

4.3 Boiler Alkaline Boil-Out Alternative

Traditional boiler boil-out uses NaOH + Na₂CO₃ at high temperature, producing wastewater with pH >13 that requires large amounts of acid for neutralization. A compound scheme using Sodium Gluconate + Na₂CO₃ + enzyme cleaning reduced alkalinity to pH 11-12 with equivalent chelating-dispersing performance. Wastewater can be neutralized to pH 8-9 using CO₂ before discharge, cutting treatment costs by 60%.

5. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: What is green industrial cleaning? How does it differ from traditional acid cleaning?

Green industrial cleaning uses biodegradable, low-toxicity or non-toxic cleaning agents and processes that reduce environmental and operator hazards. Compared to traditional strong acids (HCl, HNO₃), green agents like Citric Acid and enzyme cleaners degrade quickly, require simpler wastewater treatment, and cause lower corrosion rates on equipment substrates — particularly important for stainless steel and copper alloys sensitive to Cl⁻.

Q2: Can biodegradable cleaning agents replace all traditional cleaners?

No single agent can replace everything. For CaCO₃ scale and rust, biodegradable acids like Citric Acid perform well. However, for silicate scale, CaSO₄ deposits, and high-temperature coking residues, NaOH, EDTA or high-pressure water jetting remain necessary. In practice, a "green-first, traditional-supplement" hybrid strategy based on deposit analysis is most effective.

Q3: Are biodegradable cleaning agents more expensive than traditional ones?

Unit prices are 20%-50% higher, but total cost can be lower: wastewater treatment costs drop by over 60%, no large quantities of caustic for neutralization are needed, and lower corrosion risk reduces repair and replacement costs. For large heat exchangers, green solutions are 5%-15% cheaper overall. Companies also gain ESG scoring benefits.

Q4: What industrial equipment is suitable for enzyme cleaning?

Enzyme cleaners excel at removing organic deposits (oil, protein, biofilms) and are ideal for food processing lines, dairy plate heat exchangers, pharmaceutical reactors, beverage filling equipment, and cooling tower fill. Key limitations are temperature (40-60°C optimal) and longer contact time (2-6 hours). They are commonly used in food and pharmaceutical industries for pretreatment or intermittent cleaning.

Q5: How to evaluate the quality of green cleaning agents?

Evaluate from three dimensions: biodegradation rate data (OECD 301 standard, ≥60% = readily biodegradable, ≥70% = ultimately biodegradable); third-party corrosion rate test reports confirming rates are within standards for target materials; and supplier MSDS with heavy metal content reports. Prioritize products with green chemistry certifications or from ISO 14001-certified manufacturers.

Q6: What is the current state of green cleaning technology adoption in China?

Pharmaceutical, food & beverage, and electronics/semiconductor industries have taken the lead in adopting green cleaning solutions at scale, driven by GMP and HACCP requirements. Chemical, power, and steel industries are transitioning from pilot to broader deployment. China's "dual carbon" strategy is accelerating this shift — biodegradable cleaning agent penetration in industrial cleaning is expected to rise from approximately 15% today to over 30% within 3-5 years.
