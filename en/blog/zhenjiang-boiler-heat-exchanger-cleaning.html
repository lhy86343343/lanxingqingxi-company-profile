1. Zhenjiang Industrial Profile & Cleaning Demands

Abstract: Zhenjiang, a key chemical and power industry hub along the Yangtze River in Jiangsu Province, is home to major facilities including Jianbi Power Plant and SOPO Chemical. 1. Zhenjiang Industrial Profile & Cleaning Demands

Zhenjiang sits on the south bank of the Yangtze River, forming a vital part of Jiangsu's riverside chemical industry belt and energy infrastructure. Key facilities include Jianbi Power Plant (a backbone power station for the East China Grid), SOPO Chemical, and Zhenjiang Chimei Chemical. The extensive boiler and heat exchanger assets operate under high utilization rates. Surface water from the Yangtze River, with its elevated silt content and hardness, leads to progressive deposition of carbonate scale, silicate scale, and iron oxide on heat transfer surfaces.

Field data shows that a mere 1mm scale layer increases boiler fuel consumption by 5–8% and reduces heat exchanger transfer coefficients by 30–50%. For chemical plants, process-side fouling adds organic compounds such as polymers and tar residues, forming composite scales far more tenacious than simple calcium-magnesium deposits. Industries along the Zhenjiang chemical corridor — producing caustic soda, acetic acid, and styrene — introduce highly corrosive process media, demanding precision in cleaning formulation and corrosion inhibition. At Jianbi Power Plant, silicate and iron oxide accumulation on water-wall tubes and superheater tubes not only degrades efficiency but also risks localized overheating and tube rupture. Regular professional cleaning of boilers and heat exchangers in Zhenjiang is thus both an energy-saving measure and a critical safety safeguard.

2.2 Heat Exchanger Challenges

Shell-and-tube and plate heat exchangers in Zhenjiang chemical plants face three primary challenges: cooling-water-side scaling on the shell side reducing heat transfer efficiency; process-side polymer and tar deposition causing flow-path blockages on the tube side; and chloride-induced stress corrosion cracking risk in stainless steel exchangers. Plate heat exchangers, with narrow flow gaps (typically 2–5 mm), foul 2–3× faster than shell-and-tube types, demanding more frequent cleaning intervention.

For silicate scale common in Zhenjiang power plant boilers, EDTA sodium salt cleaning is the preferred approach. EDTA forms stable chelates with Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, and Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺, effectively dissolving silicate deposits at elevated temperatures (120–140°C) with descaling rates exceeding 95%. Post-cleaning passivation with NaNO₂ or Na₃PO₄ forms a dense protective film on metal surfaces to prevent secondary corrosion.

3.2 Heat Exchanger Cleaning Protocols

Shell-and-Tube Heat Exchangers: Shell-side (cooling water) cleaning uses LX-903 (sulfamic-acid-series compound cleaner) circulation with dedicated corrosion inhibitors for copper tube protection. Tube-side (process) cleaning employs solvent selection based on deposit analysis — tar and polymer fouling is first softened using alkaline surfactant soaking, followed by high-pressure water jetting (500–800 bar) for tube-by-tube bundle cleaning. For severely coked exchangers, a soak-and-circulate alternating method is employed: 8–12 hours soaking, circulation at 0.5–1.0 m/s flow velocity.

Plate Heat Exchangers: Disassembly and individual plate inspection, gasket condition assessment. Cleaning solution: LX-903 (sulfamic-acid-series compound cleaner) or LX-900 series (organic-acid compound cleaner, for stainless steel plates), 50–60°C, 2–4 hours circulation. Post-cleaning: individual high-pressure rinse (200–400 bar), visual inspection for residue, reassembly, and hydrostatic pressure testing.

3.3 Process Safety Controls

Real-time monitoring of cleaning solution pH, iron ion concentration, and corrosion inhibition rate throughout the operation. Minimum corrosion inhibition rate: ≥98% for carbon steel, ≥99% for stainless steel. Spent cleaning solution is neutralized to pH 6–9, flocculated, and settled before compliant discharge. Heavy-metal or organic-laden specialty waste is entrusted to licensed third-party disposal.

5. Local Case Study

Equipment: A 20 t/h industrial steam boiler (model DZL20-1.25-AII) at a Zhenjiang chemical enterprise, operated for 5 years without comprehensive chemical cleaning. Drum waterside scale thickness: 2–3 mm.

Cleaning Approach: Composite cleaning with LX-903 (sulfamic-acid-series compound cleaner) + LX-900 series (organic-acid compound cleaner), supplemented with dedicated corrosion inhibitors. Circulation at 55–65°C for 8 hours. Stubborn localized silicate deposits treated with EDTA sodium salt soaking for 12 hours.

Results: Descaling rate 96.7%, no over-pickling or pitting observed, corrosion inhibition rate 99.1%. Post-cleaning endoscopic inspection confirmed bare metal surface on the drum waterside, with no residual deposits at welds or expansion joints. Boiler thermal efficiency recovered from 78.5% to 86.2%, exhaust gas temperature dropped by 18°C, saving approximately 300 tonnes of coal annually. Six-month follow-up monitoring confirmed stable boiler water chemistry — pH, phosphate, and chloride all within specification. The client now schedules preventive chemical cleaning every two years as part of the annual maintenance plan.

7. Zhenjiang Boiler & Heat Exchanger Cleaning FAQ

Q1: How often should a boiler be cleaned?

It depends on water quality and duty. With Yangtze River water of elevated hardness, industrial steam boilers in Zhenjiang are generally cleaned every 2–3 years; with softened or demineralized water this can extend to 4–5 years. The most reliable check is opening the manhole during annual overhaul — if scale exceeds 1 mm, it is time to clean before thermal efficiency drops.

Q2: Can chemical cleaning damage the boiler?

Not with proper procedures. We use dedicated corrosion inhibitors throughout — carbon steel inhibition above 98%, stainless steel above 99% — followed immediately by passivation to form a protective film. The danger comes from unlicensed "fly-by-night" cleaning where acid is dumped in and left unattended; that is what causes pitting. Choosing a qualified company matters.

Q3: Does a heat exchanger have to be disassembled for cleaning?

Not necessarily. Shell-and-tube heat exchangers can be cleaned online in circulation — no head removal, no tube bundle handling; the cleaning solution is pumped through the loop, suitable for moderate scaling. If the shell side is fully blocked or plate heat exchanger gaskets are aging, disassembly is required. Our principle: solve it online whenever possible — no hot work, less time, less cost.

Q4: Can cleaning be done in winter? Will pipes freeze?

Yes. Zhenjiang winters are around -5°C, not extreme. The cleaning solution circulates at 50°C or above, so pipes will not freeze; during extreme cold snaps we blow down the lines during shutdown. Winter is actually a good season — many chemical plants and power plants have low winter loads, ideal for scheduled maintenance cleaning.

Q5: How much does a cleaning job cost?

It depends on equipment size and fouling. A 20-ton steam boiler chemical cleaning with passivation typically costs ¥20,000–50,000. Plate heat exchangers are priced by plate count — about a few thousand yuan for ~50 plates. Shell-and-tube heat exchangers are priced by heat exchange area, tens to over a hundred yuan per square meter. An on-site survey is needed for a firm quote — call 18952832843 for a free site visit and proposal.

Q6: How long does the cleaning benefit last?

With good water quality, two to three years — provided three conditions are met: first, passivation after cleaning (many cheap quotes skip this); second, daily scale inhibitor dosing to maintain water quality; third, regular boiler blowdown must not be skipped. Done properly, the effect lasts. One chemical plant boiler we cleaned still showed under 0.3 mm of scale after two years.

Q7: Are Zhenjiang chemical plant heat exchangers especially hard to clean?

Yes. Riverside chemical plants like SOPO and Chimei have exchangers fouled not only with water scale but also tar and polymers on the process side — ordinary acid washing cannot dissolve these organics. Our approach: sample and analyze the deposit composition first, soak with alkaline surfactant to loosen organics, then remove inorganic scale with LX-903 (sulfamic-acid-series compound cleaner), and finish with high-pressure water flushing. The combined process beats acid-only cleaning by a wide margin.
