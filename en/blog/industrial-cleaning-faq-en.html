Industrial Equipment Cleaning FAQ

2026-07-09  |  10 Most Common Questions · 25 Years of Field Experience  |  Author: Lanxing Qingxi — Luo Huiyong

Over 25 years in the industrial equipment cleaning industry, we receive countless inquiries daily from plant managers, maintenance engineers, and procurement professionals. The questions are remarkably consistent — from "Does my equipment need cleaning?" to "What does it cost?" and from "Will cleaning damage my equipment?" to "How often should it be done?" This article consolidates the 10 most frequently asked questions, answered with the practical expertise accumulated by Lanxing Qingxi through decades of field operations.

Q1: What industrial equipment cleaning services do you offer?

We offer three core processes — chemical cleaning, high-pressure water jetting, and organic solvent cleaning — covering virtually all industrial equipment involving heat exchange, fluid transport, or chemical reaction:

  • Heat exchangers — shell-and-tube, plate, spiral-plate, double-pipe, air coolers
  • Boilers — steam, hot water, thermal oil, waste heat recovery
  • Condensers — power plant turbine auxiliaries
  • Reactors — chemical and pharmaceutical reactor jackets and interiors
  • HVAC systems — chiller condensers/evaporators, cooling towers, fan coil units, piping
  • Piping systems — steam, circulating water, oil, material, process piping
  • Storage tanks — large carbon steel / stainless steel tanks, blending vessels
  • Membrane systems — RO reverse osmosis, UF ultrafiltration

We select single or combined processes based on deposit type (scale, rust, oil sludge, carbon deposits, silicate scale, biofilm, etc.), strictly following China's GB/T 25146-2010 national standard, with post-cleaning passivation treatment. Q2: How do I know if my equipment needs cleaning?

Evaluate across four dimensions — any two indicators suggest it's time to clean:

  1. Operating parameter anomalies — heat transfer temperature difference drops >15% from design, pressure drop rises >20% above baseline, energy consumption increasing noticeably.
  2. Output quality degradation — outlet temperature failing to meet design spec, condenser vacuum deterioration, insufficient product cooling.
  3. Equipment behavior changes — safety valves lifting frequently, pump current increasing, pipe vibration intensifying.
  4. Visual inspection — opening inspection covers reveals visible scale deposits (≥0.3 mm) or corrosion product accumulation on tube surfaces.

Delaying cleaning has serious consequences: a mere 0.5 mm scale layer reduces heat transfer efficiency by 8–12%. The thicker the scale, the harder and more expensive it is to remove — and the higher the risk of under-deposit corrosion and perforation. This is the #1 concern — the answer: when performed professionally, chemical cleaning is perfectly safe.

Q5: Chemical cleaning vs. high-pressure water jetting — which should I choose?

These are complementary methods, not competitors. Selection is deposit-driven, and the best results come from combining both:

DimensionChemical CleaningHP Water Jetting
Target depositsScale, rust, oil sludge, silicate scaleLoose debris, polymer deposits, hard blockages
Best applicationsTube-side interiors, non-removable equipment, complex flow pathsShell-side exteriors, removable bundles, large surface areas
Cleaning depthAll liquid-wetted surfaces, microscopic scale removalLine-of-sight limited; tube bundle centers may be missed
Substrate impactRequires inhibitors; minor corrosion risk if mismanagedPurely physical; no chemical corrosion
EnvironmentalGenerates liquid waste requiring treatmentRinse water only; relatively eco-friendly
Recommended comboHP water first to strip bulk deposits → chemical circulation to dissolve hard scale. Highest efficiency.

In practice, Lanxing Qingxi deploys combined processes on most projects: HP water (500–1,500 bar) rapidly clears shell-side and tube-sheet deposits, followed by chemical circulation to dissolve hard scale deep inside tube interiors. Q8: How is cleaning wastewater treated? Is it environmentally compliant?

Fully compliant. Chemical cleaning wastewater is treated strictly per GB/T 31188-2014 and GB 8978-1996 national standards:

  • Neutralization — acid waste is neutralized with alkali to pH 6–9
  • Precipitation & filtration — heavy metal ions removed via chemical precipitation; suspended solids via flocculation
  • Compliant discharge — treated effluent meets standards before release to industrial sewer
  • Licensed disposal — fluoride- or heavy-metal-bearing waste is transferred to certified hazardous waste handlers

For environmentally sensitive sites, Lanxing Qingxi recommends a physical-cleaning-first, chemical-passivation-supplement approach — using 3D rotating HP spray heads plus closed-loop circulation passivation, reducing wastewater volume by over 98% compared to traditional full-tank chemical cleaning. We can provide wastewater test reports for environmental compliance audits. Always verify a provider has a clear waste treatment plan and compliant disposal channels — this is the key differentiator between professionals and amateurs.

Q9: How do I choose a reliable industrial cleaning company?

Evaluate across five dimensions to avoid costly mistakes:

  1. Certifications — Do they hold China Industrial Cleaning Association certification and a safety production permit? No credentials = high risk.
  2. Industry experience — Do they have case studies for equipment similar to yours? Can they provide before/after photos and client references? The gap between 25-year veterans and newcomers is enormous.
  3. Technical approach — Do they insist on deposit sampling and site inspection before proposing a plan, or do they quote over the phone sight unseen? Quoting without inspection = unreliable.
  4. Safety commitment — Do they use professional inhibitors? Do they monitor corrosion rates? Do they have a waste treatment plan? If the answer to any is "no," move on.
  5. Warranty — Do they issue a written cleaning quality acceptance report? Is there a defined warranty period? If they won't put it in the contract, there's no guarantee.
