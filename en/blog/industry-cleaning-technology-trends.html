Industrial Cleaning Technology Trends & Innovations

2026-07-16  |  Industry Trends · Technology  |  Author: 丹阳蓝星清洗 罗会永

Abstract: The industrial equipment cleaning sector is undergoing profound technological transformation. Online non-shutdown chemical cleaning has matured significantly, enabling descaling during normal operations while eliminating costly production downtime. High-pressure water jet robots now perform precision cleaning autonomously in hazardous environments. Biodegradable green cleaning agents are gradually replacing traditional strong acids and alkalis to meet increasingly stringent environmental regulations. AI and IoT monitoring systems enable fouling prediction and intelligent cleaning-window recommendations. Under China's dual-carbon targets, post-cleaning energy efficiency recovery has become a critical lever for carbon reduction. This article systematically analyzes five key technology trends, providing technical selection references and practical guidance for industrial equipment managers.

1. Online Non-Shutdown Chemical Cleaning Reaches Maturity

Traditional industrial equipment cleaning requires shutdown, draining, and cooling before work can begin — for chemical plants, power stations, and steel mills operating continuously, each cleaning shutdown represents economic losses ranging from hundreds of thousands to millions of yuan. In recent years, online non-shutdown chemical cleaning technology has achieved breakthrough progress and become one of the most closely watched directions in the industry.

The core principle of online cleaning involves injecting cleaning agents at controlled concentrations into the circulation system while equipment operates normally. Mild acids such as Sulfamic Acid or Citric Acid gradually dissolve scale layers under flow conditions, while corrosion inhibitors (such as BTA and MBT) protect the base metal from attack. Spent cleaning solution is discharged through a bypass system for neutralization and treatment, with zero impact on production throughout the process.

Online cleaning has now been successfully deployed in the following applications: power plant condenser online descaling (cooling water return temperature drops 3-5°C, vacuum improves by 1-2 kPa); central air conditioning cooling water system non-shutdown cleaning (cooling efficiency recovers 10-15%); and chemical plant heat exchanger online circulation cleaning (heat transfer temperature differential returns to design range). The key technological breakthrough lies in optimized corrosion inhibitor formulations — modern composite inhibitors can control metal corrosion rates below 0.1 g/(m²·h), well within national standards.

The advantages of online cleaning extend beyond eliminating downtime losses to include extended equipment operating cycles, reduced labor costs, and lower cleaning frequency. However, the technology demands rigorous cleaning program design, online water quality monitoring, and equipment material compatibility assessment — requiring professional cleaning teams to develop customized solutions based on equipment metallurgy, scale deposit analysis, and water chemistry parameters.

2. Automated High-Pressure Water Jetting and Robotic Cleaning

High-pressure water jet cleaning (pressure range 500-2,800 bar), with its advantages of zero chemical residue and broad applicability, has become a mainstream industrial cleaning process. Traditional high-pressure water operations rely on manual lance handling — operators must enter confined spaces or work at height, posing safety risks while delivering inconsistent cleaning uniformity. In recent years, automated high-pressure water jet equipment and specialized robotic systems are reshaping this landscape.

Tube bundle cleaning robots represent a typical automation application: the robot carries high-pressure nozzles that auto-position along the tube sheet, using vision recognition systems to clean each tube individually with ±1 mm positioning accuracy, achieving 3-5× the cleaning efficiency of manual methods. For heat exchanger tube bundles, condenser tube plates, and reactor vessel interiors with regular geometries, robotic solutions have already achieved commercial deployment.

Additionally, wall-climbing robots and magnetic-adhesion robots demonstrate unique advantages for cleaning large storage tank and tower vessel interiors. Magnetic-adhesion robots can walk stably on vertical or inverted metal surfaces, carrying rotating nozzles for full 360° coverage cleaning, integrated with vacuum recovery systems that simultaneously collect wastewater and scale debris, achieving closed-loop zero-discharge operation.

Another direction in automated cleaning is CNC high-pressure water systems — using 3D modeling to pre-program cleaning paths, servo-driven nozzle positioning follows trajectories for precise cleaning of complex curved surfaces (such as impellers and agitator blades). These systems store cleaning parameters (pressure, flow rate, traversal speed, standoff distance), ensuring consistent cleaning quality and repeatability across identical equipment models.

3. Green Cleaning Agents Replacing Traditional Chemicals

Traditional industrial cleaning relies heavily on strong acids such as HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃, and HF, as well as strong alkalis like NaOH — posing health risks to operators, incurring high wastewater treatment costs, and presenting environmental pollution risks to surrounding areas. Against the backdrop of continuously tightening national environmental policies, green cleaning agents have become a central focus of industry R&D.

Biodegradable organic acids are a key component of green cleaning agents. Citric Acid, derived from natural fermentation, biodegrades into CO₂ and water after cleaning, with minimal impact on aquatic ecosystems. Sodium Gluconate serves as a chelating dispersant, forming stable complexes with Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, and Fe³⁺ while maintaining excellent scale inhibition under alkaline conditions, with biodegradation rates exceeding 98%.

Enzyme-based cleaning agents represent another frontier. Lipases break down oil and grease deposits, proteases target protein-based contaminants, and cellulases degrade polysaccharide biofilms. Enzyme cleaners work effectively at low to moderate temperatures (30-60°C), avoiding the energy consumption and safety risks of high-temperature chemical cleaning. In food & beverage and pharmaceutical industry CIP (Clean-In-Place) systems, enzyme cleaners have begun partially replacing traditional alkali-wash + acid-wash processes.

Natural solvent-based cleaners such as d-Limonene are also finding application in specific scenarios, offering strong dissolution capacity for organic deposits like oil residues, tar, and asphalt — and derived from citrus peel extraction, they qualify as renewable resources. The challenge for green cleaning agents lies in higher cost (typically 1.5-3× that of traditional acid cleaning), but with scaled production and tightening environmental regulations, their cost-performance ratio is improving rapidly.

4. Digital Monitoring and AI Predictive Maintenance

Industrial equipment cleaning has long relied on experiential judgment — veteran operators decide "it's time to clean" based on rough changes in inlet-outlet temperature differential and pressure drop. This approach is inherently lagging: either cleaning happens too early, causing unnecessary downtime, or too late, after severe fouling has already compromised efficiency. Digital monitoring and AI predictive maintenance are transforming cleaning from "reactive response" to "proactive prevention."

IoT sensors form the foundational layer of digital cleaning. Temperature, pressure, and flow sensors installed at heat exchanger inlets and outlets capture real-time operating data uploaded to cloud platforms. By analyzing the trend in overall heat transfer coefficient (K-value), the system can issue early warnings at the initial fouling stage (when heat transfer efficiency drops 5-8%), alerting operations teams to schedule a cleaning window. Compared to traditional reliance on manual inspection and experiential judgment, data-driven early warning can compress cleaning response time from "weeks" to "hours."

AI algorithms play an increasingly important role in cleaning decisions. Machine learning models trained on historical operating data can predict the growth rates of different scale types (CaCO₃ scale, CaSO₄ scale, silica scale, biofouling sludge, etc.) under varying water chemistry conditions, and automatically recommend optimal cleaning time windows aligned with production scheduling. Practice at a large petrochemical enterprise shows that AI-assisted cleaning scheduling can maintain average heat exchanger thermal efficiency above 85% (compared to only 70-75% under traditional methods), delivering annual energy savings in the tens of millions of yuan.

Digital twin technology is also penetrating the industrial cleaning domain. By building virtual mirror models of equipment, different cleaning approaches (chemical vs. high-pressure water, alternative chemical formulations, varying operating parameters) can be simulated in digital space to assess their impact on equipment life and performance, providing owners with quantified, comparable decision support that reduces trial-and-error costs.

5. Energy-Saving Cleaning Driven by Dual-Carbon Goals

The announcement of China's "3060 Dual-Carbon Goals" has accelerated industrial energy conservation and emissions reduction. Industrial equipment cleaning, as a direct means of energy efficiency recovery, has been endowed with new strategic significance — cleaning is no longer merely a routine maintenance activity but a crucial lever for energy conservation and carbon reduction.

Data shows that every 1 mm increase in heat exchanger scale thickness reduces heat transfer efficiency by 8-12%, corresponding to a 5-10% increase in fuel consumption. For a 600 MW coal-fired power unit, condenser fouling causing a 1 kPa vacuum drop increases coal consumption by approximately 3 g/kWh, adding roughly 12,000 tonnes of annual carbon emissions. Timely chemical cleaning to restore design vacuum can achieve substantial carbon emission reductions from this single measure alone. This transforms industrial cleaning from a "cost center" into a "carbon reduction contributor."

Waste heat recovery equipment cleaning is another energy-saving hotspot. In coking, steel, and cement industries, waste heat boiler tube bundles accumulate scale during long-term operation, drastically reducing heat recovery efficiency. Professional chemical cleaning combined with high-pressure water flushing can restore waste heat recovery rates to over 90% of design, equivalent to annual standard coal savings of 500-2,000 tonnes per waste heat boiler.

The cleaning industry's own low-carbon transition is also accelerating. Low-temperature cleaning agents (effective at ambient to 40°C) reduce steam heating demand; wastewater recovery and regeneration systems decrease chemical consumption and effluent discharge; electric high-pressure pumps replacing diesel-driven units reduce on-site carbon emissions. These green construction technologies are becoming core competitiveness indicators for cleaning service providers.

6. Conclusion: Technology Convergence Driving Industry Upgrade

The industrial equipment cleaning sector is at a critical transition point from "labor-intensive" to "technology-intensive." The five major technology directions — online non-shutdown cleaning, robotic hydroblasting, green cleaning agents, AI intelligent monitoring, and dual-carbon energy-saving cleaning — are not developing in isolation but converging and co-evolving: AI monitoring detects fouling severity → triggers online cleaning protocol → robots execute high-risk zone operations → green chemicals complete the cleaning → digital twin verifies results → automated carbon reduction report generation. This closed loop is moving from concept to reality.
