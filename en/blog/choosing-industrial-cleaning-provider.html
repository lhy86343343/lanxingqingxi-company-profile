5 Key Criteria for Selecting an Industrial Cleaning Service Provider

July 11, 2026 · Cleaning Technology  |  Author: Danyang Lanxing Qingxi, Luo Huiyong

Abstract: Industrial equipment cleaning is essential for chemical, power generation, pharmaceutical, and other manufacturing sectors, directly affecting operational efficiency and equipment lifespan. However, the market is fragmented with uneven service quality — incomplete certifications, improvised chemical formulas, and insufficient safety investment are common. Choosing the wrong provider can lead to equipment corrosion, perforation, and unplanned shutdowns. This article presents a systematic five-dimension evaluation framework — certifications, technical capability, engineering experience, safety management, and after-sales support — to help procurement and facility managers quickly identify reliable service providers.

1. Certifications and Credentials: The Non-Negotiable Baseline

Certification is the most fundamental entry barrier. Three categories warrant close inspection:

Industry Association Certification. The Industrial Cleaning Enterprise Qualification Certificate issued by the China Industrial Cleaning Association (ICAC) has four tiers (A/B/C/D), covering chemical cleaning, high-pressure water jetting, PIG cleaning, and other specialties. Grade B and above is required for medium-to-large industrial cleaning projects. Certificate authenticity can be verified on the ICAC official website — always check the tier, specialty, and validity period.

Work Safety Permit. Industrial cleaning involves strong acids, high-pressure equipment, and confined space operations, qualifying as high-risk construction work. Legitimate providers must hold a Work Safety Permit issued by housing/emergency management authorities. ISO 45001 occupational health and safety certification is a plus.

Personnel Certifications. On-site operators should hold ICAC-issued Industrial Cleaning Operator Certificates. Project managers need the Industrial Cleaning Project Manager Certificate. For high-pressure water jetting above 70 MPa, operators also require the High-Pressure Water Jetting Certificate.

2. Technical Capability: The Core Competitive Moat

While certifications answer "can they do it," technical capability determines "how well." Evaluate across three sub-dimensions.

2.1 Cleaning Process Portfolio

A mature provider should cover both chemical and physical cleaning methods. For chemical cleaning, they need formulation systems spanning acidic cleaning (HCl, Sulfamic Acid, Citric Acid), alkaline cleaning (NaOH, Na₂CO₃), and chelating cleaning (EDTA, Citrate), with the ability to adjust formulations based on deposit analysis. For physical cleaning, they should possess high-pressure water jetting equipment (50–150 MPa range), pipeline PIG systems, and dry ice blasting capability.

2.2 Laboratory Analysis Capability

The scientific rigor of a cleaning plan depends on accurate deposit characterization. A qualified provider takes deposit samples before construction, analyzes the proportions of CaCO₃, SiO₂, Fe₂O₃, and organic matter, and formulates the cleaning agent accordingly. During cleaning, they monitor pH, iron ion concentration, and turbidity in real time to determine the endpoint. Providers without analysis capability rely on guesswork — neither cleaning efficacy nor safety can be data-validated.

2.3 Corrosion Inhibition and Protection

The fundamental principle is "remove deposits without damaging equipment." Confirm that the provider has inhibitor formulations tailored to different materials — carbon steel, stainless steel, copper, titanium, aluminum — such as BTA + Urotropine combinations for carbon steel or Sodium Molybdate anodic passivation for stainless steel. Corrosion inhibition rates must meet industry standards (carbon steel ≥99%, stainless steel ≥99.5%), with specific data documented in the project proposal.

3. Engineering Experience: Judge by Cases, Not Marketing

Industrial cleaning is a practice-intensive service; experience determines a provider's ability to handle complex operating conditions.

Similar Equipment Track Record. If cleaning a shell-and-tube heat exchanger, request at least three similar project case studies with equipment parameters (material, heat exchange area, deposit thickness), cleaning methodology, and post-cleaning data. Ask to contact one or two past clients for telephone verification.

Industry Coverage Breadth. Deposit characteristics vary by industry — chemical plants face CaCO₃ + organic mixed fouling, power plants contend with silicate scaling, and coking plants deal with tar deposits. Providers with cross-industry experience adapt better to unfamiliar fouling types. Company age is also a reference — those with 10+ years of continuous operation have survived multiple industry cycles.

Emergency Response. When sudden equipment failure demands urgent intervention, can the provider arrive within 24 hours? Do they maintain chemical inventory? How many crews can be dispatched? Include response time and penalty clauses in the contract.

4. Safety Management: The Invisible Highest Cost

Industrial cleaning involves multiple high-risk factors. Providers that underinvest in safety may quote lower prices, but an accident's cost — production downtime, liability claims — far exceeds the upfront savings.

Work Procedures. The provider should supply a project-specific Construction Organization Plan or Cleaning Work Plan that identifies risk points and control measures for each process step. For confined space operations, the plan must detail gas detection, ventilation, supervision, and emergency rescue procedures.

Personal Protective Equipment. On-site personnel must wear acid/alkali-resistant gloves, splash goggles, chemical protective suits, hard hats, and anti-slip boots. High-pressure water jetting operators also need cut-resistant PPE — 300 MPa water jets can instantly penetrate human tissue causing severe injury. Verify completeness and expiration dates of all PPE before work begins.

Emergency Preparedness. A written emergency response plan covering chemical spills, personnel burns, equipment anomalies, and fire scenarios is required. Eyewash stations, first-aid kits, and fire extinguishers should be present on site.

5. After-Sales Assurance: Cleaning Is Not a One-Time Transaction

Cleaning effectiveness often takes 1–3 months to fully validate — whether heat exchange efficiency recovered, whether corrosion is controlled, whether deposits re-form. Without after-sales support, you bear the downstream risk alone.

Acceptance Criteria and Warranty. The contract should specify post-cleaning acceptance metrics: heat exchanger temperature differential recovery rate, pipeline flow recovery rate, surface cleanliness grade. Reputable providers typically offer 6–12 month warranties, with free rework if quality issues arise during the warranty period.

Periodic Follow-up and Data Tracking. Strong providers establish client equipment files and conduct follow-up visits at 3, 6, and 12 months after cleaning, tracking equipment operating data (temperature differential, pressure drop, flow rate), and recommending the next cleaning window. This reflects a preventive maintenance mindset, not a one-off transaction.

Waste Liquid Disposal Compliance. Chemical cleaning generates hazardous waste (acids, heavy metals). The provider should present legal waste treatment records or third-party disposal authorization. Illegal dumping can expose the client to environmental liability by association.

6. Summary: A Five-Step Evaluation Method

We recommend the following standardized evaluation process for selecting an industrial cleaning service provider:

  1. Credential Verification — Check ICAC qualification tier and specialty, Work Safety Permit, and personnel certificates. Eliminate any provider with incomplete credentials before proceeding further.
  2. Technical Scoring — Evaluate cleaning process portfolio breadth, laboratory analysis capability, and corrosion inhibition systems. Require a deposit-specific formulation proposal for your project.
  3. Case Verification — Demand at least three similar equipment case studies, and contact past clients when possible. Company age and industry coverage breadth serve as supplementary indicators.
  4. Safety Audit — Review the work plan, PPE checklist, emergency procedures, and insurance certificates. Any safety deficiency warrants elimination.
  5. Contract Terms — Define acceptance criteria, warranty period, waste disposal responsibility, and penalty clauses. Never rely on verbal promises.

Industrial equipment cleaning is a highly specialized technical service. Choosing a provider with complete certifications, robust technical capability, proven experience, rigorous safety practices, and reliable after-sales support protects not only your equipment assets but also your production continuity investment.
