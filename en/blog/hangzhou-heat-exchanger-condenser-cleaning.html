1. Hangzhou's Industrial Landscape and Cleaning Demands

Abstract: Hangzhou is a Yangtze River Delta hub for the digital economy, biomedicine, and automotive manufacturing, with dense clusters of data center facilities, pharmaceutical fermentation lines, and vehicle and component factories. In these scenarios, plate and shell-and-tube heat exchangers, power plant condensers, and chiller condensers commonly develop scale, biological slime, rust, and oil deposits after long-term operation, reducing heat transfer efficiency, raising energy consumption, and lowering condenser vacuum. This article introduces Hangzhou heat exchanger and condenser chemical cleaning and high-pressure water jetting solutions covering descaling, passivation, and pre-filming, to help enterprises restore heat transfer performance, stabilize production, and cut costs.

1. Hangzhou's Industrial Landscape and Cleaning Needs

Hangzhou, the capital of Zhejiang Province and a core city on the southern wing of the Yangtze River Delta, stands out for its digital economy, biomedicine, and automotive and component manufacturing. Binjiang and Yuhang host a large number of internet and cloud computing companies with dense data center facilities, where chilled water systems, plate heat exchangers, and cooling towers run at high load around the clock. Qiantang (Dajiangdong) and Xiaoshan are home to vehicle and component plants whose paint lines, mold cooling, and oil cooling systems rely on numerous heat exchangers. Pharmaceutical and fine chemical enterprises in Xiaoshan and Fuyang use fermenters, distillation columns, shell-and-tube heat exchangers, and condensers as core heat transfer equipment. In addition, the condensers of gas-steam combined cycle units at Banshan Power Plant face scaling and microbiological corrosion on the circulating water side.

After long-term operation, calcium carbonate and sulfate scale plus biological slime deposit on tube walls and plate surfaces, reducing the heat transfer coefficient by 10–30%, lowering condenser vacuum, and raising terminal temperature difference — directly increasing power and operating costs. Regular chemical cleaning and high-pressure water jetting are key to restoring heat transfer performance, extending equipment life, and ensuring safe operation.

2. Technical Cleaning Solutions

2.1 Chemical Cleaning of Heat Exchangers (Plate / Shell-and-Tube)

For calcium carbonate, rust, and biological slime deposits, acid cleaning formulations based on LX-903 (sulfamic-acid-series compound cleaner) or LX-900 series (organic-acid compound cleaner) are used with dedicated corrosion inhibitors to control metal corrosion rates (carbon steel ≤5 g/(m²·h), copper ≤1.5 g/(m²·h)), followed by neutralization, passivation, and pre-filming. Plate heat exchangers are cleaned by plate disassembly to thoroughly clear dead corners in the corrugations; shell-and-tube heat exchangers use circulation cleaning, with high-pressure water jetting for blocked tube bundles.

2.2 Condenser Cleaning and Vacuum Recovery

For power plant condensers and large chiller condensers, rubber-ball cleaning combined with chemical cleaning is used on the circulating water side, or high-pressure water jetting flushes copper/titanium tubes one by one during shutdown to remove scale and slime; film-forming protection is applied to tube bundles where necessary. After cleaning, condenser terminal temperature difference drops and vacuum recovers, markedly improving power generation and refrigeration efficiency.

2.3 Online Cleaning and Water Treatment for Data Center Chilled Water Systems

Data centers demand extremely high continuity. Online no-shutdown cleaning is used: chelating agents and dispersants are dosed to soften scale and strip slime through circulation, combined with side-stream filtration. After cleaning, pre-filming prevents rust, and a regular water quality monitoring program is established to prevent secondary scaling and Legionella growth, ensuring uninterrupted cooling all year round.

5. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How often should heat exchangers be cleaned?

Generally every 1–2 years, depending on water hardness, concentration ratio, and operating conditions. For high-hardness circulating water, high concentration ratios, or coking-prone process sides, annual cleaning is recommended. Monitor the temperature difference and inlet/outlet pressure drop daily: when the temperature difference shrinks markedly or pressure drop rises over 20%, scaling is already affecting heat transfer — schedule cleaning early rather than waiting for full blockage.

Q2: How much vacuum loss indicates a condenser needs cleaning?

A vacuum drop of 2–3 kPa below design, or a terminal temperature difference (the gap between exhaust steam temperature and circulating water outlet temperature) rising above 3°C, is a typical signal of scaling and fouling. Rising circulating water temperature rise and climbing back pressure also indicate scaled tubes. Clean promptly to avoid rising coal or power consumption and potential unit load reduction.

Q3: Does online cleaning affect normal production?

No. Online no-shutdown cleaning is designed for data centers, continuous chemical lines, and other scenarios that cannot stop production. Chelating agents and dispersants are dosed to soften scale and strip slime while equipment runs normally. For heavy scaling requiring thorough removal, a short planned shutdown is scheduled to minimize production impact.

Q4: Will chemical cleaning corrode the equipment?

No. Formulations include dedicated corrosion inhibitors to keep carbon steel corrosion below 5 g/(m²·h) and copper/copper alloys below 1.5 g/(m²·h), far below national standard limits. After cleaning, neutralization, passivation, and pre-filming further protect metal surfaces against rust and secondary corrosion.
