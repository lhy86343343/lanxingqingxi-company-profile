Chemical Cleaning Wastewater Treatment & Environmental Compliance

August 11, 2026 · Cleaning Technology & Standards  |  Author: 丹阳蓝星清洗 罗会永

Abstract: Chemical cleaning is an essential method for industrial equipment maintenance, but the wastewater generated contains acids, alkalis, heavy metals, and organic solvents that pose serious risks to water bodies and soil if discharged untreated. This article systematically categorizes chemical cleaning wastewater types and their key pollutants, details mainstream treatment processes—including neutralization, coagulation precipitation, and advanced oxidation—and their applicable scenarios, and provides practical guidance on wastewater treatment and environmental compliance in line with GB/T 25146-2010 and national environmental regulations.

1. Introduction

Industrial chemical cleaning involves substantial quantities of acid, alkali, passivation, and inhibitor solutions. The resulting wastewater is complex in composition, with extreme pH values, high COD, and elevated heavy-metal concentrations. Under the stringent enforcement of China's Water Pollution Prevention and Control Law and the Integrated Wastewater Discharge Standard (GB 8978-1996), chemical cleaning wastewater treatment has become a critical component of corporate environmental compliance. A single chemical cleaning operation on a large shell-and-tube heat exchanger can generate 20–50 m³ of wastewater containing high concentrations of Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺, and Cu²⁺ ions, along with residual pickling agents, corrosion inhibitors, and surfactants. Inadequate treatment can cause acidification, eutrophication, and heavy-metal contamination of receiving waters.

2. Wastewater Classification and Pollution Characteristics

Chemical cleaning wastewater varies significantly in composition depending on the target equipment, fouling type, and cleaning formulation. Based on cleaning media and process stages, wastewater can be classified into five major types:

Acid pickling and chelating cleaning wastewater present the greatest treatment challenges due to high heavy-metal content and elevated COD. When carbon steel heat exchangers are cleaned with HCl, Fe²⁺ concentrations in the wastewater can reach several thousand mg/L. Upon exposure to air, Fe²⁺ rapidly oxidizes to Fe³⁺, turning the wastewater dark brown with a strong pungent odor.

3. Core Treatment Processes

3.1 Neutralization

Acid-base neutralization is the most fundamental treatment step. Acid pickling wastewater is typically neutralized with lime slurry (Ca(OH)₂) or NaOH to a pH of 6–9. Lime slurry is cost-effective, and Ca²⁺ ions can simultaneously precipitate anions such as F⁻ and SO₄²⁻, providing a flocculation function. The chemical reactions are as follows:

Fe²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₂↓
4Fe(OH)₂ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 4Fe(OH)₃↓

3.2 Coagulation Precipitation

After neutralization, the wastewater still contains substantial suspended solids and colloidal pollutants requiring further treatment via coagulation precipitation. The standard approach combines polyaluminum chloride (PAC) with polyacrylamide (PAM), utilizing the adsorption-bridging effect of Al³⁺ hydrolysis products to aggregate fine particles into settleable flocs. PAC and PAM dosages must be determined through jar tests based on raw water quality.

3.3 Advanced Oxidation

For wastewater with high organic pollutant loads—such as EDTA chelating waste and streams containing inhibitors and surfactants—conventional neutralization and precipitation are insufficient. Fenton oxidation (H₂O₂/Fe²⁺) generates hydroxyl radicals (·OH) with powerful oxidizing capability, mineralizing recalcitrant organics to CO₂ and H₂O. The reaction requires acidic conditions (pH 3–4); after oxidation, pH is readjusted to precipitate the iron sludge. For cyanide-containing wastewater, alkaline chlorination with NaClO is used for cyanide destruction.

3.4 Complete Treatment Flow

The recommended full-process treatment train for HCl cleaning wastewater from carbon steel heat exchangers is as follows:

  1. Equalization — Collect wastewater from all cleaning stages, homogenize water quality and flow
  2. Neutralization — Add Ca(OH)₂ slurry, control pH to 7–8, aerate and agitate to promote Fe²⁺ oxidation
  3. Coagulation Sedimentation — Dose PAC + PAM, form and settle flocs, separate sludge
  4. Intermediate Buffering — Supernatant enters intermediate tank for pH and COD monitoring
  5. Advanced Oxidation (if needed) — Activate Fenton oxidation unit when COD exceeds limits
  6. Filtration — Sand filtration or activated carbon to remove residual SS and trace organics
  7. Compliant Discharge — Final effluent passes online monitoring before discharge or reuse for rinsing

4. Environmental Compliance and Discharge Standards

Chemical cleaning wastewater discharge must meet both national and local standards. GB/T 25146-2010, Quality Acceptance Specifications for Chemical Cleaning of Industrial Equipment, explicitly requires that cleaning wastewater be properly treated and not discharged indiscriminately. Specific discharge limits must comply with GB 8978-1996, Integrated Wastewater Discharge Standard. Key pollutant discharge limits are as follows:

Additionally, local environmental authorities are tightening oversight of heavy-metal wastewater discharges. Enterprises must maintain wastewater treatment logs with records retained for at least three years. Hazardous waste must be separately collected, stored, and entrusted to licensed hazardous waste disposal facilities in accordance with the National Hazardous Waste Inventory.

5. Waste Minimization and Green Cleaning Practices

Source reduction is the optimal path to environmental compliance. Enterprises are advised to pursue minimization across four dimensions:

Process Optimization: Adopt closed-loop circulation cleaning, where cleaning solution is recirculated after inline filtration, reducing single-operation chemical consumption by 30–50%.

Formulation Selection: Prioritize low-toxicity, biodegradable cleaning agents such as Citric Acid and Sulfamic Acid over strong mineral acids; select eco-friendly inhibitors and biodegradable surfactants to reduce treatment burden.

Online Cleaning: For operating equipment, prioritize online chemical cleaning techniques to avoid large batches of single-use wastewater from equipment shutdowns and draining.

Outsourced Management: Enterprises lacking wastewater treatment capacity should contract licensed third-party facilities, ensuring end-to-end compliance.

6. Conclusion

Chemical cleaning wastewater treatment is not optional—it is a legally mandated component of industrial cleaning projects. 7. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Does chemical cleaning wastewater require treatment before discharge?

Yes. Chemical cleaning wastewater contains acids, alkalis, heavy metals, and organic pollutants. It must undergo neutralization, coagulation precipitation, and oxidation treatment to meet GB 8978-1996 discharge standards before release. Untreated discharge is illegal and exposes the enterprise to environmental penalties.

Q2: How is acid cleaning wastewater treated?

Acid cleaning wastewater is typically neutralized with lime slurry (Ca(OH)₂) or NaOH to pH 6–9, allowing Fe²⁺ to oxidize to Fe³⁺ and precipitate as Fe(OH)₃. After coagulation, sedimentation, and filtration, the treated water meets discharge standards. High-concentration waste acid may be recycled or entrusted to licensed hazardous waste facilities.

Q3: What does GB/T 25146-2010 require regarding wastewater treatment?

GB/T 25146-2010 specifies both cleaning performance criteria and the requirement that cleaning wastewater be properly treated, with discharge quality complying with national and local environmental regulations.

Q4: How can cleaning wastewater volume be minimized?

Strategies include adopting closed-loop circulation cleaning, selecting low-toxicity biodegradable cleaning agents such as Citric Acid, implementing online cleaning technology, and optimizing formulations to minimize inhibitor and surfactant usage.
