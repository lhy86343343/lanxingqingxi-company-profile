1. Yangzhou's AC Operating Environment and Common Issues

Abstract: Yangzhou, a key industrial and commercial hub in central Jiangsu Province, hosts dense concentrations of chemical plants, machinery manufacturers, cable factories, shipyards, and automotive parts suppliers. Central air conditioning systems in these facilities operate under demanding conditions: high summer humidity, hard cooling water, and prolonged cooling seasons of up to six months. Scale buildup in condensers, biofouling in cooling towers, and dust-laden fan coil units are persistent challenges. This article provides a comprehensive overview of central AC cleaning and maintenance strategies tailored to Yangzhou's operating environment, covering absorption chillers, screw/centrifugal chillers, cooling towers, fan coil units, and air duct systems.

1. Yangzhou's AC Operating Environment and Common Issues

Yangzhou experiences a subtropical monsoon climate with hot, humid summers and damp winters. The cooling season typically spans May through October. Surface water and shallow groundwater — commonly used for cooling tower makeup — have elevated hardness levels. Under high-temperature concentration cycles in cooling towers, calcium and magnesium carbonates readily precipitate on condenser tube surfaces, forming insulating scale layers.

Industrial zones near the Yangzhou Chemical Industrial Park (located in Yizheng) add another dimension: airborne acidic gases and particulates accelerate fin corrosion on fan coil units and promote dust accumulation in supply air ducts.

Based on years of field experience across Yangzhou, the most prevalent issues include:

Condenser scaling: Hard makeup water produces scale deposits 0.3–1.2 mm thick on chiller condenser tubes within 2–3 years of operation. Each 0.5 mm of scale reduces chiller efficiency by approximately 8–12%, directly increasing electricity consumption.

Cooling tower clogging and microbial growth: Yangzhou's warm, humid summers create ideal conditions for Legionella and other pathogens. Clogged fill media not only degrade heat rejection but also pose occupational health risks. Regulatory guidelines recommend cooling tower cleaning and disinfection at least twice per year.

Fan coil unit (FCU) fin blockage: Dust, textile fibers, and industrial particulates drawn in through return air grilles accumulate on FCU coil fins, reducing airflow by 30–50% and forcing fans to work harder for diminishing cooling output.

Air duct contamination: During Yangzhou's damp winter off-season, condensation inside idle ductwork promotes mold growth. When systems restart in spring, mold spores disperse throughout occupied spaces, triggering respiratory complaints and indoor air quality concerns.

2. Technical Cleaning Solutions

2.1 Chiller Condenser Chemical Cleaning

For carbonate scale typical of Yangzhou's hard-water conditions, we employ an LX-903 (sulfamic-acid-series compound cleaner) and LX-900 series (organic-acid compound cleaner) composite formulation with dedicated corrosion inhibitors. LX-903 delivers excellent calcium carbonate dissolution while maintaining low corrosion rates on copper tubes. Combined with a dedicated copper corrosion inhibitor, corrosion rates are controlled below 0.1 g/(m²·h). We recommend online circulation cleaning — connecting a cleaning pump skid via bypass while the chiller continues operating, preserving production continuity and utilizing waste heat to accelerate reaction kinetics.

2.2 Absorption Chiller (Lithium Bromide) Cleaning

Absorption chillers are common in Yangzhou's chemical plants and large commercial buildings. Beyond conventional condenser-side cleaning, the generator section requires attention to lithium bromide solution crystallization and internal corrosion deposits. We use a specialized neutral cleaning agent with mechanical circulation flushing to avoid acid attack on carbon steel shells. Post-cleaning, solution concentration and pH are verified, with lithium bromide and inhibitor replenishment as needed.

2.3 Cooling Tower Cleaning and Disinfection

Cooling tower maintenance involves two stages: physical cleaning followed by chemical disinfection. High-pressure water jetting at 15–25 MPa removes biofilm and sludge from fill media without damaging the fill structure. The basin and fill are then chemically disinfected via circulation to eliminate Legionella and other pathogens. Makeup water is refreshed and treated with scale inhibitor, corrosion inhibitor, and biocide to establish ongoing water quality management.

2.4 Fan Coil Unit and Air Duct Cleaning

FCU panels are removed and coil fins cleaned using compressed air with soft brushing, followed by fin coil cleaner spraying with 5–10 minute dwell time and water rinsing. Air ducts are inspected via robotic camera, then mechanically brushed or pneumatically swept. Heavily contaminated duct sections receive localized chemical fogging disinfection.

4. Local Case Study

Background: In May 2026, an automotive parts manufacturer in Yangzhou reported severe cooling capacity degradation in its assembly workshop central AC system. Workshop temperatures exceeded 26°C even during peak cooling hours, affecting precision assembly quality. The system comprised two 350 kW screw chillers, three crossflow cooling towers, and over 120 fan coil units.

Diagnosis: On-site measurements showed condenser approach temperature had risen from the normal 2.5°C to 7.8°C, confirming heavy internal tube scaling. Cooling tower fill was over 60% clogged with algae and sludge, with leaving water temperature 4.3°C above design. Approximately 40% of FCU terminals delivered less than 60% of design airflow due to fin fouling.

Solution: Online chemical cleaning of chiller condensers was performed during night hours — LX-903 (sulfamic-acid-series compound cleaner) circulation for 6 hours completely dissolved carbonate scale. Cooling towers underwent full fill removal, cleaning, and partial fill replacement with chemical disinfection. FCUs were individually disassembled for fin cleaning and filter replacement.

Results: Post-cleaning, condenser approach temperature recovered to 2.7°C. Chiller operating current decreased approximately 15%, and cooling tower leaving water temperature returned to design value. Workshop temperature stabilized at 24°C ±1°C during peak heat. The client reported approximately ¥80,000 in summer electricity savings, and precision assembly defect rates dropped from 2.1% to 0.6%.

5. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How often should central AC systems be cleaned?

It depends on operating conditions and water quality. In Yangzhou's hard-water, long-summer environment, we recommend: chiller condensers annually, cooling towers semi-annually (before summer and after autumn), and FCUs plus ductwork every 1–2 years. Facilities with dusty or corrosive atmospheres need more frequent service. The energy penalty of delayed cleaning typically exceeds the service cost several times over.

Q2: Does cleaning require system shutdown?

Not necessarily. Chiller condenser cleaning can be performed online via bypass circulation — the chiller keeps running. Cooling towers, FCUs, and ductwork do require the specific equipment to be offline during service, but we schedule work during nights, weekends, or plant shutdown periods to minimize operational disruption.

Q3: Will chemical cleaning damage copper tubes?

No. We use inhibited, mildly acidic cleaning formulations. In nearly 25 years of operation, we have never experienced a cleaning-related tube failure. Tube wall thickness is measured before cleaning, and corrosion rates are monitored in real time during the process, maintained well within national standards. Ironically, the greater risk is not cleaning: long-term scale deposits cause under-deposit corrosion that truly perforates tubes.

Q4: How much energy can be saved after cleaning?

Based on data from Yangzhou projects, chiller condenser cleaning typically reduces operating current by 10–18%. For a 350 kW chiller running 5 months per year at 10 hours daily with electricity at ¥0.8/kWh, annual savings range from ¥15,000 to ¥30,000 per chiller — not including additional benefits from cooling tower efficiency recovery and airflow restoration. The cleaning cost is usually recovered within one cooling season.

Q5: Is service from Danyang convenient for Yangzhou clients?

Our headquarters is only a one-hour drive from central Yangzhou. Pricing is based on equipment specifications and cleaning scope, not on distance — there is no travel surcharge. We maintain long-term service relationships with multiple Yangzhou clients and provide complimentary pre- and post-cleaning inspections.
