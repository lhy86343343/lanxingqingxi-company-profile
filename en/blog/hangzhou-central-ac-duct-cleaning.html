1. Central AC Cleaning Needs in Hangzhou

Located in the Jiangnan water region, Hangzhou has a warm, humid climate with hot, muggy summers and damp, cold winters, keeping central AC and ventilation systems running nearly all year. The city is also dense with office towers, shopping malls, hotels and mixed-use complexes, plus data centers and biomedical enterprises that demand strict temperature, humidity and air-quality control. Any drop in heat-exchange efficiency or contaminated ventilation ducts translates directly into higher operating costs and health risks.

Hangzhou's central AC cleaning needs fall into three areas. First, the condenser and evaporator of chillers scale up after prolonged heat exchange, reducing the energy-efficiency ratio and raising power consumption. Second, cooling towers absorb dust and breed algae and Legionella. Third, ducts accumulate dust, fibers and microbes during long-term air delivery, causing secondary indoor pollution. These three are interrelated and require systematic cleaning rather than isolated treatment.

Notably, Hangzhou is a stronghold of the digital economy and biomedicine. Data centers impose strict requirements on the stable heat exchange of precision AC units, while biomedical enterprises must regularly clean and disinfect clean-room AC systems to meet hygiene standards. Such demand calls for a professional team with chemical-cleaning qualifications and mature processes.

2. Common Questions

Over years of serving Hangzhou and surrounding clients, we have collected the questions most frequently raised by facility managers and property directors.

Q1: Does cleaning an AC unit really save electricity?

Yes. Once the condenser and evaporator scale up, heat-exchange efficiency drops and the unit raises compressor energy consumption to hold the target temperature. Regular chemical cleaning removes the scale and restores a clean heat-exchange surface, significantly recovering efficiency and cutting electricity costs, typically recouped within two cooling seasons.

Q2: How often should a cooling tower be cleaned?

Cooling towers are the core of open water systems and are directly exposed to the atmosphere, so mud, algae and bacteria breed easily. We generally recommend a thorough cleaning and disinfection every six to twelve months, with shorter intervals for high-load, hard-water or dusty locations. The focus includes packing descaling, basin desilting, water-distribution unclogging and biofouling control.

Q3: Will duct cleaning disrupt normal operations?

A mature team works in zones and sections with robotic inspection and negative-pressure dust collection to minimize disruption. For offices and malls that cannot shut down, work can be scheduled at night or on weekends; air quality improves noticeably after cleaning.

Q4: How long must the AC be shut down for cleaning?

It depends on system scale and contamination. Chemical cleaning of a single chiller or cooling tower usually finishes within hours, while deep cleaning and disinfection of an entire duct system may take one to several days. We provide a plan and schedule in advance, avoiding peak load and maintaining cooling in critical zones through staged switchover.

3. Central AC Cleaning Solutions

For Hangzhou's climate and building types, Danyang Lanxing Cleaning provides a systematic, itemized cleaning solution covering chillers, cooling towers and ducts.

(1) Chemical cleaning of chiller condenser and evaporator

After long operation, scale, rust and biological slime form on the copper-tube inner walls of condensers and the heat-exchange surfaces of evaporators. We use circulating chemical cleaning with copper-safe inhibited agents — LX-903 (sulfamic-acid-series compound cleaner) and LX-900 series (organic-acid compound cleaner) — plus a dedicated copper corrosion inhibitor to protect the tube walls. The agent dissolves scale and slime under circulation, restores heat-exchange efficiency, then the system is rinsed to neutral and passivated if necessary to prevent secondary corrosion.

(2) Cooling tower descaling and Legionella control

The focus is on packing, basin and water-distribution systems. We remove mud and algae through manual desilting combined with chemical descaling, dose biocides to control Legionella and other pathogens, and deep-clean heavily fouled packing with high-pressure water or dedicated agents to restore heat-exchange area and improve cooling performance.

(3) Duct cleaning and disinfection

Duct cleaning combines mechanical brushing with negative-pressure dust collection. We first inspect supply and return ducts with a robot to locate dust and contamination, then loosen wall deposits with rotary brushes and air whips while a negative-pressure collector catches the dust immediately to prevent re-entrainment, and finally disinfect the inner walls to suppress mold and bacteria. Before-and-after imaging of the ducts is available after cleaning.

All the above procedures follow the relevant specifications for chemical cleaning of industrial equipment, with records kept at key stages for acceptance and traceability.
